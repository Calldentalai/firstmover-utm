What is a UTM link?

A UTM link is a normal URL with tracking tags added to the end. These tags tell Google Analytics exactly where a visitor came from — which video, which email, which social post.

Without UTM
https://firstmovers.ai/labs/

GA4 shows "youtube.com - referral" but you can't tell which video sent the traffic.

With UTM
https://firstmovers.ai/labs/?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=video-description&utm_campaign=julia-channel_ai-automation

GA4 shows the exact video, placement, and campaign that drove the visit.

Important: UTM Only Works on firstmovers.ai

UTM parameters are read by Google Analytics on the destination website. This means:

Will be tracked in GA4
https://firstmovers.ai/consulting-booking/?utm_source=youtube&...

The visitor lands on YOUR site. GA4 reads the UTM parameters.

Will NOT be tracked in GA4
https://calendly.com/jake/booking?utm_source=youtube&...

This is a different website. Your GA4 cannot read it.

Rule: Always generate UTM links pointing to https://firstmovers.ai/... pages. If the final destination is an external tool (Calendly, survey, etc.), see "Tracking External Links" below.

Tracking External Links (Booking, Surveys, etc.)

For links that go to external tools (Calendly, Typeform, GHL booking, surveys), UTM won't work directly because those sites don't have your GA4. Here are two ways to track them:

A
Option A: Link Shortener (Recommended)

Use a link shortener like Short.io or Bitly. The shortener tracks every click — who clicked, when, from where — then redirects to the external tool. Works for ANY URL, not just firstmovers.ai.

External URL: https://calendly.com/jake/consultation
Short link: https://frstmvrs.link/book-jake
Result: Short.io tracks all clicks, shows geographic data, device types, referrers
B
Option B: Redirect Through Your Site

Create a page on firstmovers.ai (e.g., /book/) that automatically redirects to the external booking tool. The UTM parameters get captured by GA4 on your site before the redirect happens.

UTM link: https://firstmovers.ai/book/?utm_source=youtube&utm_campaign=...
GA4 captures the UTM data on /book/ page
Page auto-redirects to: https://calendly.com/jake/consultation
Result: GA4 knows the visitor came from YouTube, AND they went to book
Best practice: Keep booking pages on firstmovers.ai whenever possible (like /consulting-booking/). This gives you full GA4 tracking with zero extra setup. Use the link shortener approach for truly external tools you can't embed on your site.

4
Done. Data starts flowing within 24 hours.

Naming Rules (Keep It Consistent)
Always
  • Use lowercase for everything
  • Use hyphens to separate words (ai-automation)
  • Use underscores to separate parts in campaign names (julia-channel_topic_date)
  • Include a date for time-bound campaigns (2026-03 or 2026-w14)
Never
  • Use spaces (breaks the URL)
  • Use UPPERCASE (GA4 treats "YouTube" and "youtube" as different sources)
  • Use special characters (#, ?, &, =)
  • Change the naming format mid-campaign
How to Check if It's Working
1
Click your own UTM link

Open it in a private/incognito browser window so it counts as a new session.

2
Check GA4 Real-Time report

Go to Google Analytics > Reports > Realtime. You should see your session appear within 30 seconds.

3
Check Traffic Acquisition report (next day)

GA4 > Reports > Acquisition > Traffic Acquisition. Filter by your utm_source. You'll see the session with the correct source, medium, and campaign.

Link History

Every link you generate is saved in your browser's local storage. This means:

  • Your history is per device, per browser — Julia's links on her laptop won't show on Josh's computer
  • Clearing browser data will clear the history
  • Use Export CSV or Export JSON to save a backup of all generated links
  • Each team member maintains their own link history — no conflicts
